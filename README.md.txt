# Sierra Leone Disaster Risk Reduction & Preparedness

A data-driven disaster risk reduction and preparedness project focused on Sierra Leone.

The project develops and applies the **Senessie DRR Intelligence Framework (S-DRIF)**, a proposed analytical and decision-support framework designed to convert fragmented disaster-risk, preparedness, governance, socioeconomic, health-system and disaster-impact evidence into structured policy intelligence.

S-DRIF is designed to move beyond simply ranking countries or measuring risk. It aims to help decision-makers understand:

- What is happening
- What is it happening
- What does context tell us
- What do we think might happen
- What solutions can we recommend employing 
- How to take action and then monitor and evaluate said action 
---

## The S-DRIF Framework

The wider decision-intelligence architecture develops this into:

**DESCRIBE → DIAGNOSE → CONSULT → TRIANGULATE → PREDICT → PRESCRIBE → DECIDE & ACT → LEARN**

The framework is designed as a progressive process in which each stage addresses a more decision-relevant question.

### 1. Measure / Describe

Establish the country's disaster-risk, preparedness, resilience, governance, socioeconomic, health-system and disaster-impact position using appropriate evidence.

### 2. Explain / Diagnose

Investigate relationships and structural factors associated with observed preparedness and disaster outcomes.

### 3. Compare

Identify structurally comparable countries and systems rather than solely relying on geographical comparison alone to highlight potential MEL cases.

### 4. Learn

Identify countries or cases that perform better than expected and investigate what may explain their performance.

### 5. Consult

Bring in stakeholder, expert, institutional, policy and contextual knowledge (affected locals, practitioners on the ground; really centering those affected by hazards in this phase to gain a clearer picture) to test and challenge emerging interpretations.

### 6. Triangulate

Integrate quantitative, qualitative, policy, hazard, institutional and contextual evidence to strengthen or challenge the emerging assessment.

### 7. Prioritise

Identify concentrated capacity gaps and match them against relevant hazards and potential areas for intervention.

### 8. Validate

Test the robustness, credibility, relevance and limitations of the evidence and emerging conclusions.

### 9. Predict

Where sufficient evidence exists, explore future risk, impact and scenario outcomes under alternative conditions.

### 10. Prescribe

Assess candidate interventions, their expected effects, costs, feasibility, uncertainty and potential trade-offs.

### 11. Decide & Act

Translate the resulting intelligence into prioritised, sequenced and contextually feasible decisions.

### 12. Learn

Use implementation, monitoring, evaluation and new disaster/event data to update the evidence base and improve subsequent decision cycles.

---

## S-DRIF Analytical Architecture


The framework does not depend on using specific methods in every application. Analytical approaches should be selected according to the decision problem, available evidence and context. Use the analyses that make sense when needed.

---

## Sierra Leone Application

The Sierra Leone project represents the current proof-of-concept application of S-DRIF.

The preliminary evidence base brings together disaster preparedness, risk, governance, economic, health-system, inequality and disaster-impact indicators.

The work has examined preparedness position, structural relationships, peer comparators, pcapacity constraints and hazard–capacity intersections.

The next stages extend this evidence base toward prediction, intervention evaluation, policy analysis and prescriptive investment decision-making.
